HYPOTHESIS 1: IMF SLOPE CORRELATES WITH RPE
================================================================================

HYPOTHESIS:
A significant inverse correlation will exist between IMF decline and RPE—
that is, as physiological fatigue increases (more negative IMF slope), 
perceived exertion also rises.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

OVERALL CORRELATION:
A Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to examine the relationship 
between objective fatigue (IMF slope) and subjective fatigue perception 
(RPE) across cycling phases.

Results revealed a significant inverse correlation 
(ρ = -0.325, p < 0.001, n = 286).

Effect size: Moderate (|ρ| = 0.325)

PER-PHASE CORRELATIONS:
  • Warmup: ρ = 0.123, p = 0.3576, n = 58 (No)
  • SecondThreshold: ρ = -0.138, p = 0.3043, n = 57 (No)
  • VO2max: ρ = -0.339, p = 0.0105, n = 56 (Yes)
  • WanT: ρ = -0.291, p = 0.0280, n = 57 (Yes)
  • Cooldown: ρ = -0.076, p = 0.5691, n = 58 (No)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hypothesis 1 is SUPPORTED.

The significant inverse correlation indicates that participants who exhibited greater physiological fatigue (more negative IMF slopes) reported higher perceived exertion.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATISTICAL DETAILS (for methods section)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Test used: Spearman rank-order correlation (ρ)
Justification: RPE is an ordinal scale (0-10)
Alpha level: 0.05
Software: Python 3.x (scipy.stats)
