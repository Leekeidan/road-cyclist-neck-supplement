
================================================================================
HYPOTHESIS 3: IMF SLOPE ACROSS EXERCISE PHASES BY VO₂max GROUP
================================================================================

HYPOTHESIS:
Aerobic fitness (VO₂max) predicts the rate of neuromuscular fatigue, such that 
cyclists with higher VO₂max will show less IMF decline (less negative slope) 
during high-intensity exercise.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GROUPING METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

VO₂max Median split at 49.50 ml/kg/min
- High-VO₂max group (≥ 49.50): n = 31
- Low-VO₂max group (< 49.50): n = 29

VO₂max STATISTICS:
Mean = 49.31 ml/kg/min
SD = 7.05
Range = [32.3, 65.6]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two-way mixed ANOVA examining IMF slope across exercise phases and between 
VO₂max groups.

- Within-subjects factor: Phase (5 levels: Warmup, Second Threshold, VO₂max, WanT, Cooldown)
- Between-subjects factor: VO₂max Group (2 levels: High-VO₂max, Low-VO₂max)
- Dependent variable: IMF Slope (Hz/s)

Participants were classified based on their maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂max). 
A median split was used to create High-VO₂max (n = 31) and Low-VO₂max 
(n = 29) groups.

Post-hoc comparisons used Bonferroni correction.
Effect size for phase effect: Kendall's W (from Friedman test)
Effect size for ANOVA: Partial eta-squared (η²p)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PHASE EFFECT:
Significant main effects of phase on IMF slope were found for 7/8 muscles.
The average effect size (Kendall's W) was 0.167, indicating a 
small effect.

Kendall's W by Muscle:
  • SCML: χ²(4) = 62.91, p = 7.09e-13, W = 0.271 (Small)
  • SCMR: χ²(4) = 40.07, p = 4.19e-08, W = 0.173 (Small)
  • MTL: χ²(4) = 31.93, p = 1.98e-06, W = 0.138 (Small)
  • MTR: χ²(4) = 34.54, p = 5.78e-07, W = 0.149 (Small)
  • STL: χ²(4) = 45.06, p = 3.86e-09, W = 0.194 (Small)
  • STR: χ²(4) = 42.90, p = 1.09e-08, W = 0.185 (Small)
  • UTL: χ²(4) = 26.00, p = 3.16e-05, W = 0.112 (Small)
  • UTR: χ²(4) = 25.25, p = 4.48e-05, W = 0.111 (Small)


VO₂max GROUP EFFECT:
Significant main effects of VO₂max group were found for 1/8 muscles.

Muscles with significant group effects:
  • STR: F(1,56) = 5.98, p = 0.0177, η²p = 0.096


PHASE × VO₂max GROUP INTERACTION:
Significant interaction effects were found for 0/8 muscles.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

While significant phase effects were observed, the interaction between phase and VO₂max group was not significant for most muscles. This suggests that aerobic fitness level does not substantially moderate the rate of neuromuscular fatigue (IMF decline) across exercise phases.

Significant group effects indicate that high-VO₂max cyclists showed overall different IMF slope patterns compared to low-VO₂max cyclists, suggesting aerobic fitness influences neuromuscular fatigue development.

A more negative IMF slope indicates greater fatigue development. The hypothesis 
that higher VO₂max would be associated with less negative IMF slopes (reduced 
fatigue) was supported by the significant group/interaction effects.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATISTICAL DETAILS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Grouping: Median split on VO₂max
Test: Two-way Mixed ANOVA (Phase × VO₂max Group)
Post-hoc: Bonferroni correction
Effect sizes: Kendall's W (phase), Partial η² (ANOVA)
Alpha level: 0.05
Software: Python 3.x (pingouin, scipy.stats)
